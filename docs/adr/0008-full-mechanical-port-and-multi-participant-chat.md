## Context

ADR 0007 reversed the "reimplement the pane" over-reach for one routine: it made the **balloon** a faithful, cited port of `balloon.cpp`/`panel.cpp`, and the class of drift that produced (a balloon layout re-derived from a mental model, wrong for two iterations) has not recurred since. But the port stopped at the balloon. Two mechanical routines were still reimplementations from analysis:

- **Figure placement** — `render.ts` hardcodes **Guide-left / Hero-right**. That is a two-figure special case of `CUnitPanel::LayoutAvatars` (`panel.cpp:528`), which orders and scales **N** bodies (`OrderAvatars`, per-body `GetDimInfo`, normalize to `unitHeight/1.9`, sum-width shrink or head-capped zoom).
- **Panelization** — `mapper.ts` folds "one Turn = one Panel." Comic Chat instead runs `CUnitPanelPage::AddLine` (`panel.cpp:362`) per utterance, deciding whether to append the line to the current panel or start a new one (the 5-element cap, the <2-panel rule, the *speaker-already-in-panel* rule, `SM_ACTION` forcing a box panel, and the overflow-split retry).

ADR 0004 justified leaving both as semantic with one argument: **chat → narration cannot be transcribed.** Comic Chat rendered a chat between several typing humans; Zork was one player and a narrator, so panel-forming and multi-body layout — routines that assume *several participants* — had nothing to bind to.

**ADR 0006 dissolves that argument.** The Guide is now a Dungeon Master, the Hero speaks back, and (this ADR's addition) **Grues and other mobs can participate in the exchange**. The scene is once again a genuine multi-party chat — exactly Comic Chat's native domain. The premise that made panelization and N-body layout un-portable is gone, and the reimplemented versions are the remaining source of the "fix it back and forth" churn ADR 0007's process fix was meant to end. So we finish the job ADR 0007 started: **the entire mechanical layer becomes a line-by-line port**, and the semantic layer shrinks to the job that is genuinely ours.

## Decision

### 1. The whole mechanical layer is a port; the semantic layer emits chat lines

ADR 0007 §1's boundary test is unchanged ("determined by geometry → port and cite; determined by the meaning of an event → ours"). We apply it to the two routines ADR 0007 exempted:

- **Panelization is mechanical.** `AddLine`/`AddPanel`/`MakeBalloon`/`AddReaction`/`StartNewPanel` (`panel.cpp:362,229,335,436`) decide panel boundaries from element counts and speaker identity — geometry and bookkeeping, no Zork meaning. Ported to a new `web/src/comic/page.ts`, cited.
- **N-body avatar layout is mechanical.** `LayoutAvatars` + `AdjustArtToCoord` (`panel.cpp:528,1435`) place and scale bodies from their dimensions. Ported into `render.ts`, replacing the Guide-left/Hero-right hardcode, cited.
- **The semantic layer's new, smaller job:** turn one Zork `Turn` into a **sequence of chat lines** — `{ avatarId, text, mode, emotion }` per line — plus scene context (`room`, `focus`, `camera`, `kind`, `marker`). Everything genuinely ours stays ours and unchanged in kind: channel mapping (ADR 0004 §2), emotion-from-narration (§9, `affect.ts`), the camera ladder (§7, the `SHOTS` table), the Narrative Director (ADR 0005), cast selection. The mapper stops *filling named balloon slots* and starts *emitting lines*; `page.ts` (ported) turns lines into panels.

### 2. The Panel seam generalizes from two slots to N participants

The `Panel` type loses its fixed `hero`/`guide`/`caption` balloons and its single panel-level `emotion`. A panel now carries a **list of lines**, each with its own speaker (`avatarId`) and its own `emotion`. `Cast` generalizes from a hero/guide pair to a map keyed by `avatarId`, so the DM, the Hero, and any mob resolve through one path. This is the data-model change that makes `LayoutAvatars` (N bodies) and `AddLine` (N speakers) express anything; it is the literal reading of ADR 0004 §1 "Comic Chat leads on identity," now including *how many* it can seat.

### 3. Figure geometry is ported; the `art/` assets stay

We port the **placement, scaling, and zoom** of `LayoutAvatars`/`GetBodyBox` (`panel.cpp:528`, `avatar.cpp`), but each body's *pixels* still come from this project's build-time `art/` pipeline (packed avatar sheets, `figureFor`/`drawFigure`, `selectComplexPose`), unchanged and still parity-checked against `art/composite.ts`. A full port of Comic Chat's live head+torso avatar-sheet decode is **deferred** — the geometry is the value; the assets already exist.

### 4. Scope boundary: the port makes the pane N-capable; it does not author mob dialogue

This ADR delivers the **capacity** to render N participants and to form panels from a line stream. Deciding *when a Grue speaks and what it says* is semantic authoring that lives upstream in the mapper/Director — a later ADR 0006 slice, not this work. Through this change the DM↔Hero content renders exactly as it does today; a third participant is proven by a test that injects a mob line, not by shipping mob dialogue.

### 5. Strategy: in-place, tests as the oracle

The rewrite lands in place behind the existing `renderPanel`/`layoutStrip`/`Panel` interface (AGENTS.md's "incremental" is honoured at the seam, not by keeping a dead parallel copy). The recorder-based tests (`balloon.test.ts` path ops, `figure.test.ts` byte-identical join vs `art/composite.ts`, `render.test.ts` geometry) are the behavioural spec: they stay green, or a change to a ported routine is justified against its C++ source as a named divergence. Because no pixel-diff gate exists today, we add one — committed `render:panel` PNGs (seed 1234) — so future balloon/layout drift is caught automatically rather than by eye.

## Consequences

- **The pane's mechanical layer is now, in full, a cited port.** New mechanical code reads the C++ first and cites it (ADR 0007's standing rule, now covering the last two routines). Only line-content, emotion, camera, and cast selection are authored fresh.
- **`mapper.test.ts` and `render.test.ts` are rewritten to the new contract** (line list, N-body layout). The `replayPanels` oracle and its dialogue-content assertions (ADR 0006) are preserved: `Turn[] → Panel[]` is still a deterministic pure function.
- **N-participant panels are enabled but idle.** Until a mapper slice authors mob lines, panels contain the DM and Hero as before; the layout simply no longer assumes exactly two.
- **Deferred:** the full avatar-sheet figure port (§3), and mob-dialogue authoring (§4). Both are revisited when a concrete need appears.

### Phase 2 built — panelization landed (2026-07-27, v0.18.3)

`web/src/comic/page.ts` is the cited port of `CUnitPanelPage`: `AddLine:1058`, `AddReaction:1142`, `MakeBalloon:1036` (as a `BM_*` bitmask, `defines.h:63`), `StartNewPanel`(panel.h:91), `FetchSpeaker:606`, `ReplaceBody:626`, `AvatarInPanel:655`, `Clone:562`, `RemoveLastPanel:993`, `AddPanel:1029`. Four findings from building it:

1. **The pipeline order is mapper → Director → page, not mapper → page → Director.** The Director's edits (drop `refusal`, collapse `traversal` runs) now apply to the *utterance* stream; panelization runs last on what survives. Editing panels afterwards would mean dropping a panel that had already absorbed a kept line by clone-append. `Mapper.panel(turn): Panel` is therefore replaced by `Mapper.read(turn): TurnScript` (`{context, lines, reactions}`), and `Director.ingest` returns a `PanelDelta` (`{replacedLast, appended}`) rather than `Panel[]`.
2. **Clone-append needs a repaint seam.** `AddLine` *replaces* the previous panel with a grown copy, so an incremental view must drop and redraw its last panel — `PanelDelta.replacedLast`, our stand-in for `RemoveLastPanel`/`RefreshLastPanel`.
3. **Panel context on a merged panel takes the newest line's** (camera, room, focus, marker, kind, turn). A `CPanel` carries none of this, so the port has to decide; a merged panel depicts the moment of its last utterance, which is the frame the reader sees.
4. **`LayoutBalloons` is injected, not stubbed away.** `AddLine`'s overflow recursion is ported in full behind a `FitTest` seam (`fits`/`leftover`); Phase 2b supplies the real driver. The default `ALWAYS_FITS` reproduces today's behaviour exactly, so the port changed no pixels on its own — verified: 5 of the 6 production `render:panel` panels are byte-identical to their `HEAD` renders.
5. **The sixth panel is the one behavioural change, and it is faithful.** `m_panels.GetCount() < 2` (`:1079`, and `:1148`) refuses to clone-append while the page holds fewer than two panels, so the *opening* turn's caption takes panel 0 and the Hero's body panel 1. Comic Chat never noticed because its page opens with a title panel (`AddTitle`, out of scope here). Every later turn is unchanged: the caption opens a panel (`BM_ACTION`, `:1064`) and the Hero clone-appends into it, one panel per turn. Pinned by `mapper.test.ts` "splits the opening turn, and only the opening turn".

Panel counts over the corpus confirm the packing discipline is live and inert: panels ≤ turns on every walkthrough, never more (e.g. 406/406 on the full game, 12/14 on `09-take-all-surface`).

### Phase 2b built — multi-balloon layout landed (2026-07-28, v0.18.4)

`web/src/comic/balloon-layout.ts` is the cited port of the layout driver: `LayoutBalloons:855`, `GetCloudEstimate:885`, `LayoutBalloon:925`, `GetInterveningBBox:167`, `LowestPreviousBottom:213`, `AdjustRouteRgns:247`, `ForceFitBalloon:153`, and the corridor arithmetic of `CBalloon::QueryRouteRgn`/`SetRouteRgn` (`balloon.cpp:1358,1375`), over `CLabel::SplitHeight` (`balloon.cpp:777`) added to `balloon.ts`. It replaces `render.ts`'s vertical stack, and `web/src/comic/fit.ts` feeds the same driver into `page.ts`'s `FitTest` seam, so `AddLine`'s overflow recursion is now live rather than stubbed. Findings:

1. **Two constants do not survive the unit change, and reading them literally is wrong.** `Dock`'s `TOPBORDER + YBORDER + HWAVEHEIGHT` lift (`balloon.cpp:568`) exists to take the *tail and wave overhang* off a cloud box before its bottom becomes the next balloon's ceiling; our box is the text box alone, so the correction is already applied and the ported shift is **0** — carrying the literal 90 pulls each balloon back over the text above it. `DockAtTop`'s `m_bbox.Top > -250` test (`:938`) is about half a line of *the original's* text; ours is proportionally smaller against the panel, so the literal length exceeds a whole balloon's height and snaps every balloon back to the top edge. The rule ported is the one the constant encodes: **dock a balloon nothing pushed down, leave a pushed one where the intervening boxes put it.**
2. **The roll is seeded from the panel's content.** `srand(m_seed)` (`:867`) buys "always lay out this panel the same way"; a content hash (FNV-1a over speaker + text) buys the same thing *and* keeps the panel stream a pure function of the event stream, so `replayPanels` still holds. The `randfloat` call sites (width goal `:899`, straddle `:916`) are ported in place around a mulberry32.
3. **A split has two halves, and the seam only carried one.** `ForceFitBalloon` splits *in place* — `SplitHeight` replaces the balloon's own string (`balloon.cpp:826`) before `AddLine` re-adds the remainder (`:1130`). `FitResult` therefore gained `kept` alongside `leftover`; without it the panel that keeps the line keeps the *whole* text and the continuation panel repeats it.
4. **The fit test measures at the native panel size, never the viewport's.** ADR 0007 §2 made panels fixed-size and the pane reflow, so this is the faithful reading — and the necessary one: panelization that read the live width would make the panel stream a function of the window. Arrow positions there are estimated as even stage slots (the cast is an async asset the Director must not wait on); the estimate decides *whether* a panel splits, never where a balloon lands, and the renderer re-runs the driver with the real figure geometry.
5. **The renderer keeps a fallback, and it is not dead code.** A panel is drawn at whatever size the strip gives it, which can be smaller than the size the fit test cleared, so the driver can refuse at draw time. `render.ts` falls back to the old stack there rather than draw nothing. Production panels do not reach it (0 of 6 `render:panel` panels); the crowded N-body fixtures do (6 of 30 — hand-built panels carrying 4–5 balloons, which panelization would have split).

Verified: 204 web tests (was 178) — `balloon-layout.test.ts` (15 rule tests), `fit.test.ts` (6), `splitHeight`/`measureAtWidth` in `balloon.test.ts`. Production panels re-rendered and eyeballed: the caption and the Hero's balloon now sit **side by side** in the band where they used to stack, and the N=2 fixture shows both speakers' balloons over their own heads with their own tails — the arrangement the driver exists to produce.

### Phase 3 built — the pixel-parity gate (2026-07-28, v0.18.5)

`web/tools/gate.ts` renders a fixed fixture set and pins each case as **the SHA-256 of its PNG plus the geometry that produced it**; `web/test/gate.test.ts` compares against the committed golden `web/test/golden/panel-gate.json` (16 cases, ~6 KB), and `npm run gate:accept` re-writes it when a change is intended. Findings:

1. **The hash is the gate; the geometry is what makes a failure readable.** Phase 2b's layout is a seeded pure function — mulberry32 over an FNV-1a hash of the panel's speakers and text — so an edit to that hash, to a line of the mapper's prose, or to a `SHOTS` rung re-places every balloon in the strip while every rule test in the suite stays green. A hash alone would catch that and say only "something moved". `renderPanel` therefore gained an **output-only `options.trace`** (`PanelTrace`: placed bodies, balloon boxes, tail targets, line counts, the fallback flag), recorded by the code that draws rather than re-derived by the gate, so a second implementation of the placement maths cannot drift from the first.
2. **Golden images are deliberately not committed.** ~300 KB of binary per case, rewritten wholesale on every accepted re-roll, buys one thing the hash+geometry pair cannot: a visual diff. A failing run writes its PNGs to `web/tools/out/gate-actual/` (gitignored) instead, to be looked at beside a re-render of the previous commit.
3. **Every fixture is staged through `page.ts`, not hand-built.** `render-bodies.ts` builds panels directly and can hand the renderer 4–5 balloons that real panelization would have split — useful to eyeball, wrong to pin, because it fixes a panel the product cannot produce. The gate feeds lines to a `Page` carrying the real `createFitTest`, so the pinned set is what the pane would draw: 10 panels of walkthrough `01-west-of-house` (seed 1234, the completion test's seed) through the real mapper, plus 3- and 4-participant scenes over the fixture cast. Panels render at `UNIT` (400×340), the size `fit.ts` panelizes at, so what is drawn and what was panelized agree.
4. **The gate immediately found one thing: a 4-participant panel that the fit test cleared and the renderer refused** (`mob4-p02`, drawn by the `stackBalloons` fallback). Cause is the estimate §Phase 2b/4 documents — `fit.ts` puts the two speakers' arrows at even stage slots (100/300 at `UNIT`), while `layoutAvatars` puts the real face anchors further in (~140/~310), and at 140 the driver has no corridor for the first balloon. This is the known cost of estimating arrow positions, not a new defect, and it is not "fixed" here: the divergence is **pinned**, with a dedicated assertion that the *set* of fallback panels is exactly `{mob4-p02}` — so a future tightening of either side shows up as a named failure rather than one more changed hash.

Verified: 238 web tests (was 204), 139 engine, 198 IR; `tsc --noEmit` and the build clean.

- **Panelization port (`page.ts`) sequenced after mobs — a measured decision (superseded by Amendment 2026-07-24b §4 and built above).** The seam and N-body layout shipped (v0.18.0). The panelization port of `CUnitPanelPage::AddLine` (`panel.cpp:362`) was *decided* — the caption rides with its panel (a divergence from `SM_ACTION`, per ADR 0006 §2), a repeat caption or a repeat speaker starts a new panel, the 5-element cap holds, and a merged panel adopts its primary (last-speaking) turn's scene fields. But a measure over the walkthrough corpus (606 turns) found **captions on 96.7% of turns**, so cross-turn packing can only merge the **3.3% caption-less turns** while forcing a re-derivation of the 30 turn-indexed replay-oracle tests. The port's load-bearing value — the 5-element cap and packing discipline — is **latent until mobs produce multi-line turns**. It is therefore built **with the mob-dialogue slice**, not against a DM↔Hero stream where it changes almost nothing. This is the ADR 0004/0005 "measure before proposing rules" method applied to its own rollout.
